Stronger Together, Safer Forever.

The National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA), headed by the Prime Minister of India, is the apex body for Disaster Management in India. Setting up of NDMA and the creation of an enabling environment for institutional mechanisms at the State and District levels is mandated by the Disaster Management Act, 2005. NDMA is mandated to lay down the policies, plans and guidelines for Disaster Management. India envisions the development of an ethos of Prevention, Mitigation, Preparedness and Response.

The Indian government strives to promote a national resolve to mitigate the damage and destruction caused by natural and man-made disasters, through sustained and collective efforts of all Government agencies, Non-Governmental Organizations and People's participation. This is planned to be accomplished by adopting a Technology-Driven, Pro-Active, Multi-Hazard and Multi-Sectoral strategy for building a Safer, Disaster Resilient and Dynamic India.

The NDMA Logo reflects the aspirations of this National Vision, of empowering all stakeholders to improve the effectiveness of Disaster Management in India. NDMA has 5 major divisions viz. Policy & Plans, Mitigation , Operations & Communications & Information & Technology , Administration and Finance.

Do's & Don'ts

Earthquakes

✓

Repair deep plaster cracks in ceilings and foundations. Get expert advice if there are signs of structural defects.

✓

Anchor overhead lighting fixtures to the ceiling.

✓

Follow BIS codes relevant to your area for building standards

×

Do not move from where you are. 

×

Do not light a match. 

View More +

Landslide

✓

Prepare tour to hilly region according to information given by weather department or news channel.

✓

Move away from landslide path or downstream valleys quickly without wasting time.

×

Try to avoid construction and staying in vulnerable areas.

×

Do not panic and loose energy by crying.

View More +

Tsunami

✓

Know the height of your street above sea level and the distance of your street from the coast or other high-risk waters.

✓

Plan evacuation routes from your home, school, workplace, or any other place you could be where tsunamis present a risk

✓

Practice your evacuation routes.

×

Move immediately to higher ground, DO NOT wait for a tsunami warning to be announced.

View More +

Cyclone

✓

Check the house; secure loose tiles and carry out repairs of doors and windows.

✓

Keep some wooden boards ready so that glass windows can be boarded if needed

✓

Keep a hurricane lantern filled with kerosene, battery operated torches and enough dry cells

×

DO NOT venture out even when the winds appear to calm down.

View More +

Forest Fire

✓

Keep emergency contact numbers of district fire service department and local forest authorities handy.

✓

Immediately inform them in case of an unattended or out-of-control fire.

×

Do not burn stubble, municipal waste, etc. next to a forest area.

×

Do not burn dry waste in farms close to forest areas.

View More +

Rainy Season (Floods)

✓

Listen to radio, watch TV, read newspapers for weather updates.

✓

Stay away from electric poles and fallen power lines to avoid electrocution.

×

Do not allow children to play in or near flood waters.

×

Don't use any damaged electrical goods, get them checked.

View More +

Summer (Heat Wave)

✓

Wear lightweight, light-coloured, loose, cotton clothes.

✓

Get trained in first aid.

✓

Stay indoors during extreme hot and use heaters safely and properly.

×

Avoid going out in the sun, especially between 12.00 noon and 3.00 p.m.

×

Avoid strenuous activities when outside in the afternoon.

View More +

Winter (Cold wave)

✓

Keep dry. If wet, change clothes quickly to prevent loss of body heat.

✓

Drink warm water and eat hot food.

✓

Listen to radio, watch TV, read newspapers for weather updates.

×

Don't step outside early morning or late night unless necessary.

×

Don't drink alcohol. It reduces your body temperature.
